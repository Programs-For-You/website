Features

Your inbox, handled in minutes

Daily summary

Pick a time, and a card pops up in the bottom-right corner with what came in that day.

One-click replies

Choose from 2 to 4 ready-made replies, or type your own in the Other box.

Never sends without you

Every reply waits for you to press Preview & Send. Nothing goes out on its own.

Flags what matters

Emails from teachers, deadlines, and family go to the top.

Sort and clean up

Groups your inbox into School, People, and Promos, and helps clear out junk. It always asks before moving anything.

AI built into your Mac

Uses the Apple Intelligence built into your Mac, so your emails stay on your computer.

Optional: your own AI key

Want smarter replies? Add your own Claude API key in Settings.

Your personal email

Gmail, iCloud Mail, and Yahoo (Outlook soon). Personal accounts only: school and work accounts like @wiltonps.org can’t be connected.

On screen, for any email

Open any email, even school email in your browser, and click On screen. It reads that email and writes replies you copy and paste.

Instructions

How to use Email Assistant

Email Assistant is coming soon. These steps show how it will work when it launches.

Before you start

  • macOS 26 Tahoe or newer. To check, open the Apple menu → About This Mac.
  • A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later). In About This Mac, look for Chip: Apple M…
  • Apple Intelligence turned on in System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri. If you can’t turn it on, you can still use the app with your own AI key (step 9).
  • A personal email account for the daily summary: Gmail, iCloud Mail, or Yahoo. School email (like @wiltonps.org) works only with On screen (step 7).
  1. 1

    Install it

    Download the app and open it the first time by following the install guide.

  2. 2

    Connect your personal email

    Personal accounts only. School and work accounts, like @wiltonps.org, can’t be connected. To answer those, use On screen.

    Open the app, click Add personal email, and pick your email. Gmail, iCloud, and Yahoo use an app password: a special password just for this app, so your real password stays private. (Outlook support is coming soon.)

    Gmail: make an app password
    1. Make sure 2-Step Verification is on at myaccount.google.com/security.
    2. Go to myaccount.google.com/apppasswords.
    3. Type a name like Email Assistant and click Create.
    4. Copy the 16-letter password and paste it into the app.

    Use your personal Gmail (an address ending in @gmail.com). School Google accounts aren’t supported.

    iCloud Mail: make an app-specific password
    1. Go to account.apple.com and sign in.
    2. Open Sign-In and Security → App-Specific Passwords.
    3. Click to make a new one and name it Email Assistant.
    4. Copy the password and paste it into the app.
    Yahoo: make an app password
    1. Go to login.yahoo.com/account/security.
    2. Click Generate app password (or Generate and manage app passwords).
    3. Name it Email Assistant and click Generate.
    4. Copy the password and paste it into the app.
    Outlook / Hotmail: coming soon

    Microsoft no longer allows app passwords, so Outlook will use Sign in with Microsoft. It’s coming in a future update.

  3. 3

    Make sure it has access

    In Settings → Accounts, click Test access next to your email. You should see three green checks: the password is saved, it can read your inbox, and it can send replies. If one is red, remove the account and add it again with a new app password.

    If your Mac asks “Email Assistant wants to use your confidential information stored in your keychain,” type your Mac password and click Always Allow. That’s how it opens your saved app password. You may see this once after each update.

  4. 4

    Pick your summary time

    Choose when the daily card pops up, like 3:30 PM after school. You can change it anytime in Settings.

  5. 5

    Reply from the card

    When the card appears in the bottom-right corner, read the summary, then pick a reply. Or choose Other and type your own. Press Preview & Send to check it, then Send. Press Skip to move on to the next email. The card stays on your screen until you’re done or you click the ×, so you won’t forget about it.

  6. 6

    Open it anytime

    Click the envelope at the top of your screen to open today’s card. Right-click it and choose Check Email Now to get your summary early.

  7. 7

    Answer any email with On screen

    Open the email you want to answer, in any app or website. School email in Chrome or Safari works too. Click the envelope at the top of your screen, click the On screen tab, then Read email. (Or right-click the envelope and choose Reply to Email on Screen.)

    Pick a reply (or type your own under Other), click Preview & Copy, then Copy reply. In your email, click Reply and press ⌘V to paste it. Email Assistant doesn’t send these: you do.

    The first time, your Mac asks to let Email Assistant see your screen. Click Open Settings, turn on Email Assistant, and click Quit & Reopen if asked. It only looks when you click Read email, and the picture is never saved.

  8. 8

    Clean up your inbox

    Right-click the envelope and choose Sort & Clean Inbox. You’ll see your email grouped into Promos, School, and People, with newsletters already checked. Click Archive or Move to Trash, and it asks you once more before doing anything.

  9. 9

    Optional: smarter AI

    In Settings → AI, turn on Use my Claude API key and paste a key from console.anthropic.com. The emails being summarized (and, for On screen, the picture of the window you asked about) are then sent to Anthropic, and Anthropic charges your API account a small amount for each, so only do this if you’re OK with that.

Your privacy: your email password is stored in your Mac’s Keychain, and your emails are read on your Mac. On screen takes one picture of the window you’re using only when you click Read email, and never saves it. We never see your email. Read the Privacy Policy.
