Features

A helper that sees what you see

One shortcut, any app

Press ⌥ Space anywhere, or click its button at the top of your screen. Press it again to close.

Sees what you see

It looks at the window you’re using (or your whole screen) and reads the words on it.

Six quick buttons

Summarize, Explain, What’s next?, Make to-dos, Write a reply, and Quiz me.

Ask anything

Ask about your screen, or about anything at all, and keep the conversation going.

To-dos and reminders

Turn an assignment into a to-do list in one click, and get a reminder before it’s due.

Spots dates and links

Due dates, links, and email addresses on your screen become buttons: remind me, open, or write an email.

Only looks when you ask

It never watches or records your screen, and it doesn’t save pictures.

AI built into your Mac

Uses Apple Intelligence, so what’s on your screen stays on your computer.

Optional: your own AI key

Add a Claude API key and it sees the actual picture, which is great for charts, diagrams, and math.

Instructions

How to use Screen Assistant

Screen Assistant is coming soon. These steps show how it will work when it launches.

Before you start

  • macOS 26 Tahoe or newer on a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later). To check, open the Apple menu → About This Mac.
  • Apple Intelligence turned on in System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri. If you can’t turn it on, you can still use the app with your own AI key (step 9).
  1. 1

    Install it

    Download the app and open it the first time by following the install guide.

  2. 2

    Let it see your screen

    The first time, your Mac asks whether Screen Assistant can record your screen. Click Open System Settings, turn on Screen Assistant under Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording, then click Quit & Reopen.

    “Record” sounds scary, but it only takes one picture each time you open it. It never records video.

  3. 3

    Open it from anywhere

    Press ⌥ Space (Option and Space together). It looks at the window you’re using and shows a small picture of it, so you know what it sees. Press ⌥ Space again or Esc to close it.

  4. 4

    Click a quick button

    Summarize gives you the key points. Explain puts it in simple words. What’s next? tells you what to do now. Make to-dos pulls out the tasks. Write a reply answers a message. Quiz me makes practice questions.

  5. 5

    Ask a follow-up

    Type in the box and press Return. It remembers the conversation until you close it or click New chat. To ask something that has nothing to do with your screen, click Using it to turn the screen off for that chat.

  6. 6

    Make to-dos and reminders

    After Make to-dos, click Add to my to-dos. Open the To-dos tab to check things off, and click the bell to get a reminder. The first time, your Mac asks to allow notifications: click Allow. Dates on your screen also show up as Remind me buttons.

  7. 7

    Look again

    Switched windows? Click the round arrow next to the picture to look at the window again or at your whole screen. You can also copy all the words on the screen from there.

  8. 8

    Make it yours

    Click the gear (or right-click the button at the top of your screen and choose Settings) to change the shortcut, look at your whole screen instead of one window, pick short or detailed answers, and open it when you log in.

  9. 9

    Optional: smarter AI

    In Settings, turn on Use my Claude API key and paste a key from console.anthropic.com. Claude sees the actual picture of your screen, so it’s better with charts and math. The picture is sent to Anthropic, and Anthropic charges your API account a small amount for each answer, so only do this if you’re OK with that.

Using it for school: Screen Assistant is great for understanding lessons, studying, and staying organized. Follow your teacher’s and school’s rules about using AI on assignments and tests, and double-check important answers, because AI can make mistakes.

Your privacy: Screen Assistant only looks when you open it, never records video, and doesn’t save pictures. With Apple Intelligence, everything stays on your Mac. Read the Privacy Policy.

Questions

It says it can’t see my screen.

Open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording and turn on Screen Assistant, then quit it and open it again. If it’s already on but still doesn’t work, select Screen Assistant in that list, click − to remove it, then open Screen Assistant and allow it again.

Pressing ⌥ Space doesn’t open it.

Another app may already use that shortcut. Settings shows a message if so. Pick a different shortcut in Settings, or click the button at the top of your screen.

It says Apple Intelligence isn’t available.

Turn it on in System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri. If it was just turned on, it may still be downloading, so try again later. Macs that can’t run Apple Intelligence can use a Claude API key instead (step 9).

My Mac sometimes asks again if Screen Assistant can see my screen.

That’s normal. macOS checks in now and then about apps that can see your screen. Click Allow to keep using it.
